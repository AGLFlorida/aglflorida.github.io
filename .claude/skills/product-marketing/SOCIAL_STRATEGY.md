# AGL Consulting Social Strategy

Based on Buffer's 7-step framework ("How to Create a Social Media Marketing Strategy in 2026", https://buffer.com/resources/social-media-marketing-strategy). Scoped to our two channels: X and the AGL Consulting LinkedIn page, both scheduled through Buffer.

Numbers marked (starter) are guesses to beat, not baselines. Replace them after the first monthly review.

## Rules for every draft

The product-marketing skill applies these to every post it writes.

- Fit a pillar (see 5). Release posts are pillar 1; if a draft fits none, say so.
- Repurpose, don't duplicate: X gets a sharp one-idea post, LinkedIn gets the longer version built for dwell time and comments.
- LinkedIn: end with a question or prompt that invites a comment when it fits naturally. Comments and dwell time drive LinkedIn reach.
- Suggest a visual: a screenshot for X, a 3-6 slide carousel (PDF document post) for LinkedIn launches. Carousels are LinkedIn's highest-engagement format.
- Tag links with UTM so the monthly review can attribute clicks:
  `?utm_source=<x|linkedin>&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=<product-id>-<version>`
- Drafts are a starting point. The founder edits in their own voice and adds one real detail (why we built it, a user story) before queueing. Human-edited posts beat fully AI-written ones.

## 2. Audience

The one person: a small-business owner or founder who needs senior technical help (an app, automation, AI adoption) but can't justify a full-time CTO. Their problem: "I know tech could fix this, but I don't know who to trust to build it."

Secondary, per product:
- VesseLog: boat owners, and crew coaching staffs running launch fleets.
- Dual N-Back / RecallKit: students and self-improvers.

The apps prove we ship. The consulting buyer is who pays. Posts about apps should quietly show the craft (accessibility, privacy, release discipline) that a buyer would want on their project.

Refine from real signals: LinkedIn comments, contact-form submissions, app reviews. As Buffer puts it, you can't know who resonates until the content is out there.

## 3. Goals (one per platform, per quarter)

| Platform | Goal | KPI (starter) |
|----------|------|---------------|
| LinkedIn | Credibility with consulting buyers | 6 posts/month averaging 500+ impressions; 2 contact-form leads/quarter attributed to LinkedIn |
| X | Drive app awareness and trials | 4 posts/month; 50 link clicks/month to product sites |

Goals without numbers are wishes. Revise these numbers after the first full month of data.

## 4. Platforms

- LinkedIn (primary): where consulting buyers make decisions. Algorithm rewards dwell time and comments. Text, carousels, and professional-but-human tone all work.
- X (secondary): rewards reactive, in-the-moment posts. Good for release news and quick build notes; poor fit for long explainers.
- Not now: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook. Fewer platforms done well beats many done thinly. Revisit only if a product (e.g. VesseLog) shows demand for video.

## 5. Content pillars

Picked where what buyers look for, what we can credibly teach, and what isn't oversaturated overlap.

1. Releases and launches: new versions of VesseLog, Dual N-Back, RecallKit, Punch-In. (This skill's main job.)
2. How we build: release automation, React Native, accessibility, privacy, honest takes on AI tooling. Source: our blog (e.g. release-automation, ai-not-quite-there).
3. Services in practice: fractional CTO, AI enablement seminars, workflow automation, shown through outcomes, not sales copy.
4. User stories: how real users use the apps (boat owners, coaching staffs).

Podcast test: if the founder were a podcast guest, these are the topics they'd talk about. Give a pillar a few posts before judging it; one flop doesn't retire it.

## 6. Calendar and cadence

Buffer's benchmarks are LinkedIn 2-5 posts/week and X 3-4 posts/day. That's not realistic for a one-person shop; Buffer itself says one post/week per platform is a good start.

| Platform | Cadence (starter) | Mix |
|----------|-------------------|-----|
| LinkedIn | 1-2 posts/week | ~1 release or user story, ~1 how-we-build or services |
| X | 1 post/week plus every release | Mostly releases and short build notes |

- Schedule in Buffer; use its suggested posting times, then adjust from our own data.
- Queue a week at a time. Check Buffer's current free-plan queue limit per channel before batching further out.

## 7. Measure and refine

- Monthly (Buffer analytics + site analytics): check KPIs from section 3. Weekly data is too noisy to act on.
- Priority metrics:
  - Awareness: impressions, reach to non-followers.
  - Engagement: shares, comments, saves (in that order).
  - Conversion: UTM link clicks, contact-form submissions, app installs.
- Quarterly: do more of what works; give underperformers more time or drop them; check we're chasing KPIs, not vanity metrics.
- No major pivots before 3 months of data.
