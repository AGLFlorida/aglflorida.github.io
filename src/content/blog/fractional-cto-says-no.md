## Why the Refusal Matters

The clearest signal of whether a fractional CTO engagement is right for a company is what happens when you describe what it covers. If the response is "that sounds like what we need," it is probably a fit. If the response is "but can you also..." it probably is not.

That is not a slight. It is a scope problem. A fractional CTO engagement works because it stays in the strategy and architecture layer. The moment it drifts into delivery management, staff coaching, or hands-on development work, it becomes something else - typically something less valuable for everyone involved.

Knowing what it refuses is the clearest way to understand what it is for.

## What It Does Not Cover

An AGL Consulting Fractional CTO engagement does not cover:

**1-on-1 coaching.** Fractional CTO work addresses how the organization makes technical decisions, not how individual engineers develop their skills. Those are different problems. Career coaching and performance feedback belong with a manager or a dedicated coaching arrangement.

**Delivery management.** Running sprints, managing tickets, chasing blockers - that is a project management or engineering management problem. A fractional CTO sets the architecture and strategy. Getting the work done inside that frame is the team's job.

**Staff augmentation.** This is not a resource you slot into an open role. It is a leadership engagement. If what you need is another developer or a tech lead who writes code, there are better arrangements for that.

The [service page](/products/fractional-cto) states some of this. What it does not say clearly enough is why these refusals exist - which is worth explaining.

## Why These Are Refusals, Not Gaps

Each item above is left out deliberately, not because it is too hard or too costly. It is left out because including it would dilute the engagement into something generic.

A fractional CTO engagement works at the architecture and strategy layer: technology roadmap, organizational design, build-versus-buy decisions, executive communication. Those things require time to think and time to understand the business. If that time gets absorbed by delivery management and 1-on-1s, the strategic work either does not get done or gets done badly.

The best fractional CTO engagements look like this: the company makes better technical decisions faster, their engineering team gets clearer constraints and more room to work inside them, and the founder or executive team gets someone who can explain what technology can and cannot do at the board level. None of that happens if the engagement is also managing sprints and coaching junior developers.

## Who It Is For

A fractional CTO engagement fits a specific situation. The company has outgrown ad-hoc technical decision-making. A full-time CTO hire is too much, too soon - either the stage is wrong, the runway is too short, or the need is not full-time. What they need is senior judgment available on a consistent basis, without the overhead of a full executive hire.
